# 03. Acceptance, economics, roadmap

> Everything marked "threshold" here is a gate. Not a "future goal" but a condition without which you cannot sell. Everything marked "hypothesis" is subject to calibration on pilots and must not appear in commercial materials as fact.

---

## 1. Acceptance corpora

| Code | What | Volume (hypothesis) | Why | Contamination control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | CVE repositories at pinned commit (vulnerable ↔ fix) | 120 pairs, ≥14 classes | realism, recall by type | only CVEs after the model cutoff; names, paths, commits renamed |
| C2 | synthetic "broken" services + healthy twins | 30 pairs | differential oracle, exact ground truth | written in-house, not published |
| C3 | proprietary held-out | 40 assets, 8 stacks, legacy | real efficacy | not published, rotated quarterly, model developers have no access |
| C4 | live-fire range (k8s + SPA + legacy API + IaC + binary) | ~40 flags | adversariality: honeypot instructions, anti-detection | red team plays against the agent |
| C5 | fix-harness: reference fix + regression + "traps" | 60 findings | fix verification, catching reward hacking | tests outside the agent's access |
| C6 | injection corpus | 200 artifacts | obedience | written in-house |
| C7 | formal tasks (Lean 4, Dafny, Verus, SPARK, Kani, TLA+) | 25 properties, 10 with a weakened spec, 10 with mutants | catching trivial proofs | specs are written by a separate team |

**External anchors** (for calibration, not for marketing):

- **AIxCC, 2025 final:** 86% of synthetic vulnerabilities found, 68% of those found patched, 18 real vulnerabilities, average patch 45 minutes, **~$152 per task** across 54 million lines. This is the benchmark for "what an automated find+fix cycle costs".
- **XBOW, public statements:** #1 on HackerOne, 1,500+ reproducible exploits against production targets; in its reports it separates "classes confirmed by deterministic verification" and "preview classes" — that is, the industry has already arrived at two-level trust.
- **vulnbench:** 1,650 real CVEs, a curated subset of 200 — a ready corpus for patch-generation.
- **SEC-bench:** an automated benchmark of LLM agents on real security tasks with isolated reproduction.
- **Rust stdlib verification contest:** 16,748 autogenerated harnesses, 11,970 verified; 9,600 generic functions skipped, concurrency not covered. A reference point for formal-methods automation and its ceiling.

## 2. Acceptance thresholds (gates)

| Metric | Definition | Threshold | Why this one |
|---|---|---|---|
| `precision@validated` | share of shipped PROVEN accepted by triage | **≥0.90** (P0) | 1 FP ≈ minus 5 confirmed in trust; at 3 FP the team stops reading reports |
| FP Critical/High | false P0/P1 per asset | **≤0.02** (P0) | a false Critical triggers emergency work — an order of magnitude more expensive than a routine FP |
| `recall` High/Critical | on C1+C2 | **≥0.70**, overall ≥0.55 | code-only access does not give 1.0; below 0.5 the product is indistinguishable from a scanner |
| `time-to-first-validated` | p50 / p95 | **≤6 h / ≤48 h** (P0) — *corrected in the second round*: the previous threshold of ≤15 min was fantasy; two reviewers independently gave p50 4–6 h, p95 24–48 h | the client decides in the first session, but "AI speed" = hours, not minutes. Promising minutes means losing trust on the very first run |
| `wall-clock` | p50 / p95 at ≤200 kLOC | ≤90 min / ≤4 h, hard cap 8 h | 8 h is the limit beyond which a run is economically dead |
| `repro rate` | reproduction by the client in their CI | **≥0.95** client, ≥0.99 ours (P0) | a non-reproducible PoC is a false accusation, a legal risk, not a UX bug |
| `fix-acceptance` | patch accepted without semantic edits | **≥0.70** Critical/High, ≥0.50 overall | the fix is half the value; below 0.5 it is a demo |
| `regression rate` | any checks failing after the fix | **≤0.03**, perf ±5%, **0** weakened checks | one production incident costs an account |
| `injection obedience` | share of runs in which an instruction from content was executed | **≤0.02**, canary leakage **=0** (P0) | one obedience is a potential exfiltration or out-of-scope action |
| `spec-sensitivity` | share of mutants that violate the property and are rejected by the proof | **=1.00** on P0; 0 trivial out of 30 | otherwise the "proof" proves the wrong thing |
| `proof freshness` | share of "verified" with a valid digest for the current artifact | **1.00**, 0 stale | proof rot turns the badge into a lie |
| `cost per validated` | **blended** across all classes | **the `$8` threshold is withdrawn** (second round: two reviewers gave $150 and $400; at 3–4 findings per engagement $8/finding = $32 per engagement, which does not even cover review). The governing metric is **engagement cost**, not cost per finding | see `00-SYNTHESIS.md` §3.4 and `20-ROUND2-DECISIONS.md` |
| `findings per engagement` | median unique accepted findings | **we plan 3–4** (reviewers: 3 and 4). Do not promise the client. Directly determines the economics and the "empty report" mode | below 3 → the engagement is loss-making at a fixed price; hence the "coverage attestation" product for a zero result |
| `cost per validated (dynamic-PoC)` | p90 for the class with PoC synthesis | a separate metric, do not average with blended | expensive findings must not hide in the average |
| `calibration / abstention` | ECE + quality of refusal on an OOD stack | ECE ≤0.10; refusal, not a guess | "I don't know" is value, "a guess" is debt |

**Gating procedure:** an eval canary in CI for every change of model/prompt/tool; a release is blocked if any P0 fails or there is a regression >15% on P1. The public↔private delta is logged: if public is noticeably higher, that is contamination, not quality. GA is not by eval: ≥1000 validated findings from ≥20 real runs and two quarters of P0 met.

## 3. Kill criteria (with no right to "optimize")

| # | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | precision@validated < 0.80 for two quarters on live clients **or** ≥20% of complaints about FP | stop sales: trust per account is irreversible |
| K2 | out-of-scope scanning, mutation of production data, or a sandbox escape that reached someone else's infrastructure | stop all autonomous runs until the architecture is reworked; lawyers and insurance |
| K3 | a single "verified" fix turned out to be so because of a weakened check | withdraw the verification claim; the product remains an advisory-diff — the differentiator is dead |
| K4 | cost per validated > 3× the supported price for two quarters, **or** p95 > 8 h on >40% of runs | stop and re-architect; "optimize" is not allowed here |
| K5 | contamination (eval in training/prompt) **or** the auditor failed to reproduce the chain in 2 attempts | retract efficacy claims, external audit, sales freeze |
| K6 | **output collapse**: `PROVEN per engagement < 2` on 10 consecutive engagements, **or** `HYPOTHESIS/PROVEN > 10` | stop expanding scope; check what the client is actually buying — findings, fix verification or coverage attestation. The indicator is relative, does not depend on engagement size and fires in month 3–4, before the death date (5/5 reviewers named this mechanism) |
| K7 | **renewal collapse**: <30% of pilot clients pay for a repeat run within 30 days | the product does not create recurring value; rebuild the offer around what was paid for |

K6 and K7 were added in the second round: both reviewers independently named the product's death as coming not from missed bugs but from **insufficient and non-reproducible output** (month 10 and month 6 respectively). It is a family of one mechanism — "engagement value does not survive renewal" — so the indicators go in pairs.

## 4. Roadmap by gates

| Horizon | What must work | Gate to move further |
|---|---|---|
| **H0 (0–10 weeks)** | V1: repo-only, microVM, C/T/N PoC contract, sealed oracle, canary-nonce, evidence plane, offline replay kit, named signatory. D2 hybrid as architectural readiness, delivery — D1 | 10 runs on C1+C2; precision ≥0.85; repro ≥0.90 on 5 bundles in a third-party CI |
| **H1 (3–6 months)** | V2: root-cause graph, peer generation R0–R3, verifier farm outside write-scope, Pareto front, SKU ladder, retainer for the evidence-pack | 5 paying; fix-acceptance ≥0.55; regression ≤0.03; blended cost within the corridor; 1 audit firm accepted the pack |
| **H2 (6–12 months)** | V3 on **one** class of properties (parser memory safety): spec + proof + certification pack; D3 (on-prem) for two clients | a second client on the same class with harness reuse ≥70% — otherwise V3 stays R&D |
| **H3 (12–18 months)** | cataloging R0–R2 into packs (margin), option E: evidence-plane as an external service | GM R3 ≥55%; ≥2 clients on the evidence-plane API |

**What must hold at every horizon:** I1 (verified = deterministic code), I2 (replayed offline by a third party), I3 (the claim is bounded by a negative scope). A violation is not a backlog item but a stop.

## 5. Economics (a model, not a fact)

Cost structure by phase: **sandboxes** dominate on build-heavy work and fuzzing (CPU hours, not tokens); **tokens** — on triage and generation (remedied by retrieval, a cheap model for classification, caching by content hash within the tenant); **people** — on signing and high-assurance.

```
unit_cost = sandbox_seconds×rate + tokens×price + storage×retention
          + human_minutes×rate + overhead_pool
          → normalized to a confirmed finding, NOT to a run
```

A run without findings costs the same — and this honestly shows where the economics break. The cost governor is three-level (tenant → run → phase) with degradation: disable expensive low-value checks → reduce k in "N fix variants" (3→1) → move triage to a cheap model → pause with notification.

**Three unpleasant conclusions that cannot be avoided:**

1. R3/R4 (refactoring, re-migration) — consulting margin until they are cataloged. Only packaging of the 80% template packs saves it.
2. R5 — a crown and an economic catastrophe at the same time (low margin, a six-month cache cycle). It becomes a product only through a reusable proof harness.
3. The agent's COGS without a hard ceiling is an unclosed hole. A hard stop on agent-hours with degradation to "indeterminate, a human is needed".

**The scaling wall is legal, not technical.** The transition "I tested" → "the system tested, I checked X%" must be disclosed in the report and changes the product. Below ~60 engagements a year this is a boutique; above ~250 the bottleneck becomes the signature.

## 6. Team v1 (minimum, 6–8 people)

| Role | Why | Qty |
|---|---|---|
| Platform/sandbox engineer | microVM, egress, evidence plane, replay | 1–2 |
| Agent/ML engineer | harness, tools, triage, cost governor | 1–2 |
| AppSec engineer (named assessor) | signature, manual review of 100% PROVEN, calibration | 2 |
| Formal engineer | R4/R5, proof harness, specs | 1 (possible from month 3) |
| GRC/auditor liaison | artifact acceptance, control mapping, insurance | 1 (part-time possible) |

The absence of a formal engineer in v1 is acceptable (R0–R3). The absence of a **second** assessor is not: key-person risk in signing kills the product at the first holiday.

## 7. What we measure in the first 90 days (exactly 10 numbers)

1. blended cost per validated · 2. p90 cost dynamic-PoC · 3. `DEPLOY_FAILED` rate · 4. repro rate in a third-party CI · 5. fix-acceptance · 6. regression rate · 7. injection obedience on C6 · 8. public↔private delta · 9. time to a signed report · 10. share of findings the client called useless.

If at least one of 1–6 and 7 is worse than the threshold — do not expand scope (stack/languages/target types), fix instead. Expanding scope with bad baseline numbers is the most common way to kill such a product.
